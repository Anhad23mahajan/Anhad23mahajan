# Viva cheat-sheet (say each in one or two sentences)

## Unit V: Basics of Investment
1. **Investment return:** gain (income + price rise) as a % of the money invested. Rs 10,000 becomes Rs 11,000, so the return is 10%.
2. **Compounding frequency:** at the same yearly (nominal) rate, adding interest more often gives a little more money. Yearly 11,200, monthly 11,268, continuous 11,275 (A = P e^(rt)) on Rs 10,000 at 12%. Effective annual rate = (1 + r/m)^m - 1, so 12% nominal is 12.68% monthly and 12.75% continuous.
3. **Uneven cash flows:** the amounts differ each year, so discount each one separately and add: PV = sum of CF_t/(1+i)^t. 909 + 1,653 = 2,562. This is not an annuity because the payments are unequal.
4. **Economic equivalence:** at a given interest rate, different sums at different times can have the same value. At 10%, Rs 100 today is equivalent to Rs 110 after 1 year (F = P(1+i)^n, P = F/(1+i)^n). At a different rate they would not be equivalent.

## Unit VI: Risk and Uncertainty
1. **Decision under risk:** chances are known, so pick the highest average payoff (EMV). 0.5 x 100 + 0.5 x 40 = 70. EMV ignores how risk-averse the person is.
2. **Decision under uncertainty:** chances unknown. On the Stock (80, -20) vs Bond (40, 25) table: maximax picks Stock (best of the best), maximin picks Bond (best of the worst), minimax regret picks Bond (Stock's worst regret is 45, Bond's is 40). Other rules you may be asked about: Laplace (equal chances, Stock 30 vs Bond 32.5, so Bond) and Hurwicz (a mix of optimism and pessimism).
3. **Risk premium:** extra expected return a risky asset must offer over the risk-free rate. 12% - 6% = 6%.
4. **Diversification:** spreading money over assets that do not move together lowers risk. It removes asset-specific risk only; market (systematic) risk stays, so the curve flattens but does not reach zero.
5. **Life insurance:** you pay a premium and the insurer pays the sum assured if you die within the term. Simple 1-year premium is about sum assured x P(death) = Rs 1,000 (ignoring interest, costs and profit).
6. **Endowment:** pays the sum assured if you die within the term OR survive to the end. It is term insurance plus a pure endowment (a benefit paid only on survival).

## Likely questions
- Does higher risk always mean higher return? No. Investors demand a higher expected return for taking more risk, but actual returns can be lower.
- Why can diversification not remove all risk? Market-wide risk affects every asset.
- Why is Rs 100 today worth more than Rs 100 next year? It can earn interest.
- How is endowment different from term insurance? Endowment also pays on survival.
