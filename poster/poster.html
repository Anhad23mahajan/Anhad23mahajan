UNIT V: BASICS OF INVESTMENT

1. Investment Return

Return = the gain (or loss) on an investment as a % of the money put in.
R = (V1 − V0 + Income) / V0 × 100
For many years use the CAGR (average yearly growth):
CAGR = (Vn / V0)1/n − 1
Eg: ₹10,000 grows to ₹14,641 in 4 yrs
CAGR = (1.4641)1/4 − 1 = 1.10 − 1 = 10% p.a.

4. Economic Equivalence

Different amounts at different times are equivalent if they have the same value at one common date, at the given interest rate. (₹1 today ≠ ₹1 tomorrow.)
F = P (1 + i)n    ⇔    P = F / (1 + i)n
Eg: at i = 10% p.a., all three below are equivalent:
10,000 × (1.10)2 = 12,100   |   10,000 / 1.10 = 9,090.91
Use: compare loans, pick between ₹10,000 today vs ₹12,500 in 2 yrs (12,100 < 12,500, so wait!).

2. Risk Premium

Extra return demanded for taking risk instead of a safe asset.
Risk premium = E(R) − Rf
Eg: Stock E(R) = 12%, T-bill Rf = 6% ⇒ premium = 6%
Required return = Risk-free rate + Risk premium

4. Life Insurance

A contract: you pay a premium, the insurer pays the sum assured if the insured dies (term plan). Many people pool their risk: the loss of the few is paid by the premiums of the many (law of large numbers).
Net single premium (1-yr term) = S × q / (1 + i)
Eg: S = ₹1,00,000, q = P(death in the year) = 0.01, i = 5%
NSP = 1,00,000 × 0.01 / 1.05 = ₹952.38
Types: Term (death only), Whole life (death, any time), Endowment (death or survival). Actual premium = NSP + expenses + profit.

5. Endowment Assurance

Pays the sum assured if you die within the term OR survive to the end: it is insurance + saving.
Endowment = Term insurance + Pure endowment (survival benefit)
Eg: 2-yr plan, S = ₹1,00,000, q = 0.01 each yr, i = 5%
NSP = 1,00,000 [ 0.01/1.05 + 0.0099/1.052 + 0.9801/1.052 ]
= 1,850 + 88,898 = ₹90,748 (mostly the savings part)
