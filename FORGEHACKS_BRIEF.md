## 6. What to build and what to submit

### What to build
A **working AI-powered project** that addresses a real-world problem within one of the six tracks. It should demonstrate clear use of AI/ML (models, APIs, agents, computer vision, NLP, recommendation systems and so on) and show how it could create tangible value for users or communities.

### What to submit (all required)
1. **Project title** and a **short description** (clear problem and solution).
2. **Track selection**: one of the six.
3. **Public demo video, 2 to 4 minutes maximum**, posted online (for example on YouTube). It must show:
   - the problem you are solving, and
   - how the project works.
4. **GitHub repository** with source code and a **clear README**.
5. **Written project description** covering:
   - problem statement and target users,
   - technical approach and technical components used,
   - how the solution creates real-world impact.
6. **Screenshots, an architecture diagram, or a deployment link** for testing.

> **Incomplete submissions (missing video or code) will not be eligible for judging.**

## 7. Rules in full (condensed from the Rules tab)

**Project and submission requirements**
- You may use open-source libraries, public datasets and pre-trained models.
- No projects that violate laws, promote harm, or infringe intellectual property.
- **All team members must be listed on the Devpost submission.**
- **Code must be publicly viewable** (or privately shared with organizers if required for judging).
- **AI coding tools** (Copilot, ChatGPT and similar assistants) **may be used** to help build the project. This is separate from the project's own use of AI/ML, which is scored under Technical Implementation.
- **Projects must be substantially created during the hackathon period.** Pre-existing projects are not eligible unless it is clearly stated what was added during the event.
- **One submission per team.** If a team submits several projects, only the most recent one is judged.
- The rules do **not** say anything about submitting the same project to another hackathon. They only require that the work was created in the hackathon period.

**Ownership**
- Teams keep full ownership and IP of their projects.
- By submitting you give organizers and sponsors a non-exclusive, royalty-free licence to display, reference and promote the submission (name, description, screenshots, video).

**Other**
- Judging is by a panel of mentors and industry or academic judges. Ties are broken by the panel. Judging decisions are final. Organizers can disqualify rule or Code of Conduct violations.
- Participation is voluntary, with the usual liability disclaimer.
- Zero tolerance for harassment, discrimination or toxic behaviour (removal from the event and Discord).
- Online only: all collaboration, mentorship and submission happen virtually.
- Rules may be corrected or clarified by the organizers. Material changes are announced through Devpost updates and/or Discord. Questions about how the rules apply go in the **Discussions** tab.

## 8. Judging criteria

| Criterion | What judges look for |
|---|---|
| **Real-World Impact & Relevance** | How clearly the project addresses a genuine problem. Potential for actual use or meaningful benefit to users and communities. **Must answer the prompt.** |
| **Technical Implementation & AI Use** | Quality of the AI/ML components, technical depth, correctness, and thoughtful integration of AI (**not just a wrapper**). |
| **Innovation & Creativity** | Originality of the idea or approach. Fresh combinations of technology or novel application. |
| **Execution & Completeness** | Working demo, polish, usability, and how much was actually shipped during the hackathon. |
| **Presentation & Communication** | Clarity of the video, README and written description. Ability to explain the problem, solution and impact simply. |

The five criteria are not given weights.
